P1 Energy — Visual Walkthrough

Stores, transfers, efficiency and resources, one figure at a time.

Energy is the accounting system of physics. Nothing is created and nothing is destroyed, so every question in this topic comes down to the same move: name the store the energy was in, name the store it ended up in, and work out how much made the trip. This page walks P1 in that order, from a single push through to how a country powers itself. Read it top to bottom the first time, then use the jump links to find the one figure you have forgotten.

These figures come from an American textbook, so they say potential energy where AQA says gravitational potential energy store, and nonconservative force where AQA says dissipation. The physics is identical. The resource data in the last section is from 2006 and 2011, so treat the shape of the pie as the lesson and not the numbers.

1 · Energy stores and systems

Specification P1.1.1

A system is an object or group of objects. When a system changes, there are changes in the way energy is stored within it. Everything else in this topic is a special case of that one sentence.

Back to top

2 · Changes in energy

Specification P1.1.2

Three stores carry nearly every mark at GCSE: kinetic, gravitational potential and elastic potential. Each has an equation, and each equation counts the work that went in.

Back to top

3 · Energy changes in systems

Specification P1.1.3

Heating is the third way to change a system. How much the temperature rises depends on three things, and the specific heat capacity is the one that belongs to the substance.

Back to top

4 · Power

Specification P1.1.4

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred, or the rate at which work is done. An energy transfer of one joule per second is a power of one watt.

Back to top

5 · Conservation and dissipation of energy

Specification P1.2.1

Energy can be transferred usefully, stored, or dissipated, but it cannot be created or destroyed. Dissipated energy is not gone. It is stored in less useful ways, which is what the word wasted means.

Back to top

6 · National and global energy resources

Specification P1.3

The last part of P1 asks you to compare resources: renewable against non-renewable, how each is used, which are reliable, and what their use costs the environment.
