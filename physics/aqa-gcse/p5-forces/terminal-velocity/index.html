Terminal Velocity

A skydiver's fall in five stages: watch the air resistance grow until it balances the weight, twice.

A skydiver of mass 80 kg steps out of an aircraft and falls for 25 s, then opens a parachute. Follow the two forces on the skydiver, weight and air resistance, and the velocity–time graph they produce.

Press Play, or click a letter to jump to that stage. The weight arrow stays the same length all the way down. The air resistance arrow changes with the speed. The resultant force is the difference between them, and it decides what the velocity does next.

Curves and flats

Curved sections: A, B, D

  • The forces are unbalanced, so there is a resultant force.
  • A resultant force means an acceleration: the velocity changes.
  • In A and B the resultant force is downwards, the same way as the motion: speeding up.
  • In D the resultant force is upwards, opposite to the motion: slowing down.
  • The gradient of the graph is the acceleration.

Flat sections: C, E

  • Air resistance equals weight: the forces are balanced.
  • The resultant force is zero, so the acceleration is zero.
  • The skydiver keeps falling at a constant velocity.
  • This constant velocity is the terminal velocity.
  • The gradient of the graph is zero.

The weight is 784 N in every stage. Everything that happens on the graph comes from the air resistance changing.

Where you have met this before

On the Rocket's Journey graph, stages 5 and 6 were two flat lines below the axis.
