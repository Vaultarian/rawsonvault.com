What a scale measures

The reading is the normal contact force

  • A scale measures how hard you push down on it, and shows that force as its reading.
  • Newton's Third Law says those pushes come in a pair. You push down on the scale, so the scale pushes up on you with a normal contact force, FN, of the same size.
  • scale reading = normal contact force on you

Two forces act on you

  • Your weight pulls down and the normal contact force pushes up.
  • Standing still, the two forces balance, so the scale reads your weight.
  • When the forces on you are unbalanced, the reading changes. That reading is your apparent weight.

The four lift cases

The reading follows the resultant force on you, which points the same way as your acceleration.

Normal

At rest, or moving at a steady speed, up or down.

Resultant: zero

FN = W

Heavier

Speeding up going up, or slowing down going down.

Resultant: up

FN = W + m × a

Lighter

Speeding up going down, or slowing down going up.

Resultant: down

FN = W − m × a

Zero

Free fall: the cable has gone and weight is the only force.

Resultant: down, = W

FN = 0

The weight never changes. Only the push from the scale changes, and that push is the reading.
