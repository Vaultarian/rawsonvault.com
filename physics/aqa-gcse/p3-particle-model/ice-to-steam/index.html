Ice to Steam

The whole heating curve: five stages, and in each one either the kinetic energy or the potential energy rises.

0.50 kg of ice at −10 °C is heated steadily until it has all become steam at 110 °C. Follow the kinetic energy and the potential energy of the particles through every stage.

Press Play, or click a letter to jump to that stage. On the sloping parts of the curve (A, C, E) the temperature rises. On the flat parts (B, D) the state changes. The first two stages on their own: Ice to Water.

Slopes and flats

Sloping sections: A, C, E

  • Temperature rises.
  • Particles move faster, so kinetic energy increases.
  • Potential energy stays the same.
  • Energy: ΔE = m c Δθ, with the specific heat capacity of the state the water is in.

Flat sections: B, D

  • Temperature stays the same while the state changes.
  • Particles keep the same speed, so kinetic energy stays the same.
  • Energy breaks bonds between particles, so potential energy increases.
  • Energy: E = m L, fusion for melting (B), vaporisation for boiling (D).

Internal energy is the total kinetic and potential energy of the particles, so it increases in every stage.

How much energy does each stage take?

For 0.50 kg. Specific heat capacity: ice 2100, water 4200, steam 2000 J/kg °C. Specific latent heat of water: fusion 334 000 J/kg, vaporisation 2 260 000 J/kg.
