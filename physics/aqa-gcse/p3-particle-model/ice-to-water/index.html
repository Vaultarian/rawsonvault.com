Ice to Water

Warming ice from −5 °C to water at 0 °C is two jobs, not one.

A block of ice at −5 °C is heated until it has all become water at 0 °C. What happens to the internal energy of the particles during the whole process?

Two stages, two marks

A · Warming the ice, −5 °C to 0 °C

  • Temperature rises.
  • Particles vibrate faster about fixed positions, so kinetic energy increases.
  • Bonds stay intact, so potential energy stays the same.
  • Energy: ΔE = m c Δθ (specific heat capacity of ice)

B · Melting at 0 °C

  • Temperature stays at 0 °C.
  • Particles keep the same speed, so kinetic energy stays the same.
  • Energy breaks bonds and the particles slide past each other, so potential energy increases.
  • Energy: E = m L (specific latent heat of fusion)
Show a full answer
  1. From −5 °C to 0 °C the temperature of the ice rises, so the kinetic energy of the particles increases.
  2. At 0 °C the ice melts. The temperature stays the same while bonds between the particles break, so the potential energy of the particles increases.

The internal energy (the total kinetic and potential energy of the particles) increases throughout: through Ek in stage A, then through Ep in stage B.

How much energy does each stage take?

For 0.50 kg of ice. Specific heat capacity of ice = 2100 J/kg °C; specific latent heat of fusion of water = 334 000 J/kg.
