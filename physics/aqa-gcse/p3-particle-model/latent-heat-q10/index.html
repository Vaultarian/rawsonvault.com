3.5 Specific Latent Heat · Question 10

Snowball Hits a Wall

The snowball's kinetic energy becomes potential energy of its particles, and some snow melts.

Higher · one energy chain, written as one equation
Calculate the mass of snow that melts when a 280 g snowball at 0 °C hits a wall at 25 m/s and stops. Assume all of its kinetic energy is used to melt snow. Give your answer in grams.

A moving or falling object that stops can turn its Ek or Ep (macro) into internal energy (micro). Write the chain as one equation, e.g. ½ m v² = m L: the whole object's mass on the left, the mass that melts on the right.

One chain, two links

Macro · the whole snowball

  • The snowball moves at 25 m/s, so it has kinetic energy.
  • Use the mass of the whole snowball, in kg: 280 g = 0.280 kg.
  • Energy: Ek = ½ m v²

Micro · the particles

  • The snow is at 0 °C and stays at 0 °C.
  • The energy breaks bonds, so the particles' potential energy increases.
  • Here m is the mass that melts: E = m L, so m = E ÷ L

Working, one step at a time

These steps use the question's own values. Try each line on paper, then reveal it.

  1. Chain½ m v² = m L   (whole snowball on the left, mass that melts on the right)
  2. Convert280 g = 280 ÷ 1000 = 0.280 kg
  3. EquationEk = ½ m v²
  4. SubstituteEk = ½ × 0.280 × 25²
  5. AnswerEk = 87.5 J
  6. RearrangeE = m L, so  m = E ÷ L
  7. Substitutem = 87.5 ÷ 334 000
  8. Answerm = 0.000 26 kg
  9. In gramsm = 0.26 g
