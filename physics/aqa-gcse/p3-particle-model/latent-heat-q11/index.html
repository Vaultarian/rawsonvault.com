3.5 Specific Latent Heat · Question 11

The Falling Hailstone

The hailstone's gravitational potential energy becomes potential energy of its particles, and part of it melts.

Higher · one energy chain, written as one equation
Calculate the percentage of a 4.0 g hailstone at 0 °C that melts when it falls 2500 m. Assume all of its gravitational potential energy is used to melt it. g = 9.8 N/kg.

A moving or falling object that stops can turn its Ek or Ep (macro) into internal energy (micro). Write the chain as one equation, e.g. m g h = m L: the whole object's mass on the left, the mass that melts on the right.

One chain, three links

Macro · the whole hailstone

  • The hailstone starts 2500 m up, so it has gravitational potential energy.
  • Use the mass of the whole hailstone, in kg: 4.0 g = 0.0040 kg.
  • Energy: Ep = m g h

Micro · the particles

  • The ice is at 0 °C and stays at 0 °C.
  • The energy breaks bonds, so the particles' potential energy increases.
  • Here m is the mass that melts: m = E ÷ L
  • Percentage = mass that melts ÷ mass of hailstone × 100

Working, one step at a time

These steps use the question's own values. Try each line on paper, then reveal it.

  1. Chainm g h = m L   (whole hailstone on the left, mass that melts on the right)
  2. Convert4.0 g = 4.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0040 kg
  3. EquationEp = m g h
  4. SubstituteEp = 0.0040 × 9.8 × 2500
  5. AnswerEp = 98 J
  6. RearrangeE = m L, so  m = E ÷ L
  7. Substitutem = 98 ÷ 334 000
  8. Answerm = 0.000 29 kg = 0.29 g
  9. Percentage0.29 ÷ 4.0 × 100
  10. Answer7.3 % of the hailstone melts
