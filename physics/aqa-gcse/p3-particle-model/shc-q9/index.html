The Immersion Heater

Energy first, then the temperature rise, then the final temperature.

Calculate (Higher) the final temperature of 1.5 kg of water, starting at 20 °C, after an immersion heater has transferred 2000 J every second to it for 90 s. Give your answer to 2 significant figures.

Three steps to the final temperature

The route

  • Energy: 2000 J every second for 90 s. Multiply to find the energy transferred.
  • Temperature rise: ΔE = m c Δθ is on the equation sheet. Divide both sides by m c.
  • Final temperature: add the rise to the starting temperature, 20 °C.
  • Write the final answer to 2 significant figures.

Ek · Inside the water

  • The temperature of the water rises steadily while the heater is on.
  • The particles move faster, so their kinetic energy increases.
  • The water stays a liquid, so potential energy stays the same.
  • Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg °C, from the table.

Energy transferred: 2000 × 90 = 180 000 J.

Δθ = ΔEm c = 180 0001.5 × 4200 = 180 0006300 = 28.6 °C.

Final temperature = 20 + 28.6 = 48.6 ≈ 49 °C.
