Ice Block to Water

Find the mass of the ice, carry it across, then find the volume of the water.

Calculate the mass of a block of ice of volume 0.0050 m³ (density of ice = 920 kg/m³), and then the volume of the water formed when it melts in a sealed container (density of water = 1000 kg/m³).

This is two calculations joined by one idea. First find the mass of the ice. The container is sealed, so that mass is carried across to the water. Then find the water's volume.

Both containers hold the same number of particles. The dashed line on the right marks the volume the ice filled.

Two stages, one mass

Stage 1 · the ice

  • Known: volume and density of the ice.
  • Find the mass: m = ρ V
  • Use the density of ice, 920 kg/m³.

Stage 2 · the water

  • Sealed container: the mass is conserved, so the water has the mass from stage 1.
  • Find the volume: V = m / ρ
  • Use the density of water, 1000 kg/m³.

The working, one step at a time

The density equation ρ = m / V is recalled from memory: it is not on the equation sheet. Both stages use it, rearranged.

  1. Stage 1 · equationρ = m / V, rearranged for mass: m = ρ V
  2. Stage 1 · substitutionm = 920 × 0.0050
  3. Stage 1 · answer with unitm = 4.6 kg
  4. Carry the massMass is conserved, so the water has m = 4.6 kg.
  5. Stage 2 · equationV = m / ρ
  6. Stage 2 · substitutionV = 4.6 / 1000
  7. Stage 2 · answer with unitV = 0.0046 m³
  8. CompareSmaller than the ice: the particles have moved closer together.

m = ρ V = 920 × 0.0050 = 4.6 kg. Mass is conserved, so the water has m = 4.6 kg: V = m / ρ = 4.6 / 1000 = 0.0046 m³. Smaller than the ice: the particles have moved closer together.
