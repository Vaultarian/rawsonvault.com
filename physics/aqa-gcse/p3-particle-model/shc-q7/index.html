Copper and Water

The same energy, two very different rises.

Calculate the temperature rise of (a) 1.0 kg of copper and (b) 1.0 kg of water when each is given 8400 J of energy.

Same energy in, different rise out

(a) Copper

  • Specific heat capacity = 385 J/kg °C, from the table.
  • Each kilogram takes 385 J to warm by 1 °C.
  • 8400 J gives a rise of 21.8 °C (about 22 °C).
  • The particles vibrate much faster: a large increase in their kinetic energy.

(b) Water

  • Specific heat capacity = 4200 J/kg °C, from the table.
  • Each kilogram takes 4200 J to warm by 1 °C.
  • 8400 J gives a rise of 2.0 °C.
  • Water stores a large amount of energy for every degree it is heated.

(a) copper: Δθ = 84001.0 × 385 = 21.8 °C (about 22 °C).

(b) water: Δθ = 84001.0 × 4200 = 2.0 °C.
