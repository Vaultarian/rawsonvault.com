3.5 Specific Latent Heat · Question 12

Hot Brake Discs

The car's kinetic energy becomes kinetic energy of the particles in its iron brake discs.

Higher · one energy chain, written as one equation
Calculate the temperature rise of a car's brake discs when the car, of mass 1200 kg, brakes from 20 m/s to a stop. All of its kinetic energy is transferred to the iron brake discs, of total mass 6.0 kg. c of iron = 450 J/kg °C.

A moving or falling object that stops can turn its Ek or Ep (macro) into internal energy (micro). Write the chain as one equation, e.g. ½ m v² = m c Δθ: the car's mass on the left, the brake discs' mass on the right.

One chain, two links

Macro · the whole car

  • The car moves at 20 m/s, so it has kinetic energy.
  • Use the mass of the car: 1200 kg.
  • Energy: Ek = ½ m v²

Micro · the particles in the discs

  • The iron stays solid: every bond intact.
  • The particles vibrate faster, so their kinetic energy increases and the temperature rises.
  • Use the mass of the discs, 6.0 kg: ΔE = m c Δθ, so Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c)

Working, one step at a time

These steps use the question's own values. Try each line on paper, then reveal it.

  1. Chain½ m v² = m c Δθ   (the car on the left, the brake discs on the right)
  2. EquationEk = ½ m v²
  3. SubstituteEk = ½ × 1200 × 20²
  4. AnswerEk = 240 000 J
  5. RearrangeΔE = m c Δθ, so  Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c)
  6. SubstituteΔθ = 240 000 ÷ (6.0 × 450)
  7. AnswerΔθ = 89 °C (to 2 s.f.)
