56 North

A three-stage sailing programme built around the DofE framework — from the Tay estuary to the open ocean

56 North is named for the latitude of St Andrews. Its design principle is simple: each stage is genuinely harder than the last. Students who complete the pathway go from never having sailed a dinghy to being ready for offshore passage-making with an experienced professional crew. The progression follows the IB year groups: MYP students begin on sheltered estuary water; DP students step up to coastal sailing on the Firth of Forth; university students join 59 North for bluewater expeditions.

The programme maps onto the Duke of Edinburgh's Award at every level — sailing satisfies the Physical section, sailing theory the Skill section, and multi-day passages the Expedition section. For Gold, a five-day offshore passage with 59 North Expeditions satisfies the Residential requirement in full: it is purposeful, structured, and conducted with people not previously known to the student.

Stage 1 · MYP · Years 7–9

Royal Tay Yacht Club — Broughty Ferry

Tay estuary. Sheltered, tidal, ideal for beginners. RYA Level 1 and 2 dinghy sailing. DofE Bronze: Physical section. ~25 min from St Andrews.

Stage 2 · DP · Years 12–13

Port Edgar Yacht Club — South Queensferry

Firth of Forth. Coastal, tidal, real conditions. Yacht-based — bigger boats, bigger stakes. DofE Silver/Gold: Physical + Expedition. ~50 min from St Andrews.

Stage 3 · University

59 North Expeditions — Offshore

Offshore passage-making with Andy Schell's professional crew. Students completing the full pathway are passage-ready. Gold DofE Residential: 5-day offshore passage satisfies all criteria.

The programme is in development. Stage 1 (RTYC MYP dinghy programme) and Stage 2 (PEYC DP yacht programme) are the primary design focus for the 2026–27 school year at St Leonards. The 59 North partnership is the long-term aspiration for students who complete the full pathway.
