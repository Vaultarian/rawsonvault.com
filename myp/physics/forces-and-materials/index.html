Forces & Materials

An introduction to the forces at work all around us, met first through observation and demonstration

This Year 8 unit introduces forces through direct observation before formalising them. Its centrepiece is a forces circus — a set of hands-on demonstration stations where students confront a puzzle at each one and have to explain it: a cup that will not fall, rings that refuse to touch, a ball held aloft on a jet of air, a boat that floats, a charged rod and cloth. From these starting puzzles the unit develops the language of gravity, friction, upthrust, air pressure and static electricity.

The unit gives students an early, tactile feel for what a force is and how forces interact, laying groundwork that the Year 9 Mechanics unit later makes quantitative.

What students learn

  • Recognising and naming the forces acting in everyday situations — weight, friction, upthrust, air resistance and applied pushes and pulls
  • Why dropped objects fall, and what holds objects up against gravity
  • Static electricity, demonstrated with a rod and cloth, and why like charges repel
  • Floating, sinking and upthrust, and air pressure effects such as a ball suspended in a stream of air

Activities & investigations

  • A forces circus of demonstration stations, each posing a "why does this happen?" question to explain

How it is assessed

The handover assessment for this unit targeted criterion A, knowing and understanding, testing students' grasp of the forces met through the circus and class work. Other criteria are confirmed against the department's task-specific clarifications.
