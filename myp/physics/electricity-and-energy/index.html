Electricity & Energy

Transforming energy and understanding the patterns of energy transfer is key to our success now and in the future

This Year 9 unit runs as a sequence of practical lessons. Students start by learning the names, symbols and uses of circuit components and building circuits from diagrams, then develop the idea of charge as a flow of electrons and learn to control current in series and parallel circuits. The second strand turns to energy: the stores we draw on to power our lives, how energy is transferred, and the patterns that govern those transfers. The unit closes on the question of using energy sustainably.

The key concept is change, with related concepts of energy, patterns and transformation, in the global context of scientific and technical innovation. It is a strongly hands-on unit, built around circuit-building kit and a set of structured lessons running from first circuits through to energy and sustainability.

Statement of inquiry: Transforming energy and understanding the patterns of energy transfer is key to our success now and in the future.

Inquiry questions include: What are the components in circuit diagrams and what do they do? What types of energy do we use to power our lives? How can we control electricity in electric circuits? How can we use energy sustainably?

What students learn

  • The names, symbols and uses of circuit components — bulb, battery, wire, switch, resistor, variable resistor, diode and more
  • Building real circuits from a circuit diagram, and drawing clear diagrams in return
  • Charge as a measure of how many electrons pass a point, and what is happening in the wires of a circuit
  • Controlling current in series and parallel circuits
  • Energy stores and transfers — identifying the store supplied and the way energy moves in real devices
  • The types of energy we use to power our lives, and the patterns of energy transfer
  • How energy can be used sustainably, and the choices that involves

Activities & investigations

  • A station-based circuit-building activity, working from diagrams to real circuits using the electricity trolley kit
  • Electricity workbooks, including an "electricity at home" strand
  • An energy circus — stations such as a kettle on a Bunsen, a battery and bulb, a dynamo, and a car on a track, where students identify the energy stores and transfers at each
  • A structured run of lessons from first circuits through charge and current to energy and sustainability

How it is assessed

The unit is assessed across the MYP Sciences criteria. The handover assessment used a criteria B and C summative — a conducting-putty investigation in which students plan, gather and evaluate data — alongside a criterion A test on circuits and energy.
