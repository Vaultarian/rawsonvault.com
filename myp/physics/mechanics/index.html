Mechanics

Movement, forces, pressure and moments — the movement of systems is due to interaction

This Year 9 unit makes motion quantitative. Students learn to describe movement with displacement-time and velocity-time graphs, to calculate speed and acceleration, and to identify the forces that change how a system moves. A recurring strand is the question of falling: whether all objects fall at the same rate once air resistance is removed, and how to design an experiment that settles it.

The key concept is systems, with related concepts of movement and interaction, in the global context of scientific and technical innovation. The unit builds directly on the Year 8 introduction to forces and prepares students for GCSE-level mechanics.

Statement of inquiry: Movement of systems is due to interaction.

Inquiry questions include: How can we describe, measure and explain motion? How do interactions cause systems to move? Do all objects fall at the same rate?

What students learn

  • Drawing and interpreting displacement-time graphs — reading stationary, constant-speed and changing-speed motion, and calculating speed from the gradient
  • Drawing and interpreting velocity-time graphs, and calculating acceleration from them
  • The distinction between distance and displacement, speed and velocity
  • Using speed = distance ÷ time and acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time
  • The factors that change a vehicle's acceleration — mass, driving force, resistive forces and slope
  • That all objects fall at the same rate in a gravitational field when friction is ignored
  • An introduction to orbits, including why planets further from the Sun move more slowly

Activities & investigations

  • Speed-time and acceleration worksheets building graph-reading and calculation fluency
  • An investigation into the acceleration of a vehicle down a ramp
  • An experiment to show that objects fall at the same rate when air resistance is removed
  • Online motion-graphing tools to link real movement to the shape of a graph

How it is assessed

The unit is assessed across the MYP Sciences criteria. The handover assessment used a criteria B and C summative built around acceleration — students plan an investigation, process their data, and evaluate the method — supported by a scaffolded version and a science-skills guide.
