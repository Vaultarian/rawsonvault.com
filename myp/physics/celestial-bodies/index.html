Celestial Bodies

Careful observation of the universe from Earth can help us understand events on Earth and beyond

This Year 8 unit, also called The Heavens, is naked-eye astronomy done properly. Students learn to describe and explain what they can actually see from the ground: the path of the Sun across the sky through the seasons, the phases of the Moon, the difference between planets and stars, and the slow wheel of the constellations through the night. The emphasis is on building a working mental model of the Earth–Sun–Moon system rather than memorising facts.

The key concept is relationships, with related concepts of movement, pattern and interaction, in the global context of scientific and technical innovation. The work rewards careful observation and clear diagrammatic reasoning — students are expected to work out, for example, where the Sun rises at a given latitude and season from first principles rather than recall.

Statement of inquiry: Careful observation of the universe from Earth can help us understand events on Earth and in the universe.

Inquiry questions include: How do the stars move in the night sky? What are the phases of the Moon, and why do they happen? Why is there summer and winter?

What students learn

  • How latitude and longitude describe position on Earth, and why higher latitudes are generally colder
  • The directions of Earth's rotation, its orbit around the Sun, and the Moon's orbit around Earth
  • How Earth's tilt causes the seasons, and the meaning of the equinoxes and solstices
  • Describing the Sun's path across the sky for different latitudes and seasons, including where it rises and sets
  • The significance of the Arctic and Antarctic Circles and the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn
  • Explaining the phases of the Moon and the terms new, crescent, half, gibbous, full, waxing and waning
  • Telling planets from stars, and the difference between inferior and superior, inner and outer planets
  • The motion of the stars through the night, circumpolar stars, and using Polaris to find your latitude
  • Recognising the Plough and Orion's Belt, and naming our galaxy, the Milky Way

Activities & investigations

  • A Stellarium task tracking the moon phases and the changing night sky on screen
  • Modelling the passage of the Sun across the sky and reasoning about seasons and day length
  • Research questions on circumpolar stars and observing the night sky
  • A criterion D task connecting astronomical observation to its real-world significance

How it is assessed

The unit is assessed against the MYP Sciences criteria, with a strong criterion D thread — reflecting on how observation of the heavens has shaped human understanding — alongside a knowledge-based end-of-unit test.
