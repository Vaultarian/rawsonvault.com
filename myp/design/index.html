MYP Design

The computer science strand at St Leonards — robotics, programming, and computational thinking, taught through the design cycle

At St Leonards, MYP Design in Years 8 and 9 is the computing strand that feeds into GCSE Computer Science (OCR J277) in Years 10 and 11. The timetable labels it Design; the content is computer science — robotics and control, programming, animation and computational thinking. Each year is anchored by one well-developed main unit and rounded out with shorter strands. Lessons run for roughly 52 minutes a week.

Design is assessed against four criteria, each marked out of 8, that follow the MYP design cycle: investigating a problem, developing ideas, creating the solution, and evaluating it.

The four MYP Design criteria

  • A — Inquiring and analysing. Investigating the problem, researching existing solutions, and writing a clear design brief.
  • B — Developing ideas. Writing a design specification, generating and presenting feasible ideas, and planning how to make them.
  • C — Creating the solution. Following a plan to build the product or program, and documenting changes along the way.
  • D — Evaluating. Testing the solution against the specification, evaluating its success, and suggesting improvements.

Year 8 — Robotics & Control

Robotics & control Main unit

The developed Year 8 unit. Students program TPBot robot cars driven by the BBC micro:bit, using MakeCode blocks and MicroPython, to complete a series of control challenges. It runs as a full design-cycle project: inquire into a control problem, design a solution, build and program the robot, then test and evaluate it against the brief. The kit lives in the school's computing room.

Computational thinking In development

A shorter strand alongside the robotics unit, drawing on Bebras-style computing challenges to build problem-decomposition and algorithmic reasoning away from a screen. An area being grown as the course is made Alex's own.

AI & typing In development

A short closing strand on AI awareness and touch-typing, sitting at the end of the year. Content and length are being settled.

Year 9 — Animations & Games

Interactive animations & games Main unit

The developed Year 9 unit and the year's heaviest coding load. Students design, build and evaluate an interactive animation or game in Python using Pygame Zero, run in the Thonny or Raspberry Pi code editor. It is a clean full design-cycle task: inquire into the genre and audience, specify and design the game, build it, then playtest and evaluate against the specification.

CS taster In development

A deliberate widening of the lens beyond coding, designed to show students what computer science is and to bridge towards the GCSE J277 choice. Computational thinking and real-world CS live here, alongside more Bebras-style challenges.

AI & typing In development

The same closing AI-awareness and touch-typing strand as in Year 8, with content and length still being decided.

The two main units, Robotics & Control and Animations & Games, are mature and inherited intact from the previous teacher. The shorter strands are being built out, and St Leonards' task-specific criteria and weighting are confirmed with the department before first assessment.
